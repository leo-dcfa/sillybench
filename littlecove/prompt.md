The place to have in mind is Little Cove at Noosa Heads: a small north-facing pocket of sand tucked in under the headland where Noosa National Park begins. Pandanus and hoop pines lean out over the beach, a cobbled apron of rounded rocks wraps around both ends of the cove, and a timber staircase drops down through the scrub onto the sand. Off to the right the point runs out into the water as a low rock shelf.

The wave is the whole point of the place: a slow, waist-to-shoulder right-hander that bends around the point and peels for a long, long way — soft-shouldered, never steep, the kind of wave that lets a rider trim, cross-step to the nose and hang five for a hundred metres. Morning glass, a light offshore holding the face up, clean lines of swell stacking out to the horizon, turquoise water over pale sand.

Show the ocean and the beach beautifully, with riders on longboards making the most of those easy waves. Make it move: swell marching in, the wave peeling down the point, foam running up the sand and draining back.
